# What goes viral and what doesn't

Measured in November 2026 on narrated Roblox story shorts ("In this town, ..." and similar): viral hits (0.2-5.6 M likes) against weak
videos of the same niche (2-40 k likes), frame by frame and in the player. Numbers are medians unless noted. The samples are small
(several rounds, the largest 12 hits against 5 weak videos): read them as strong tendencies, not laws. Notes marked "author's test" are
judgements from building test videos, not measurements of other videos.

**Read this first: the story decides.** The same channel with the same look got 140 k likes for one story and 5.5 k for the next.
Viral hits share a production baseline, and weak videos often meet it too. The baseline does not make a video viral; missing it can
make a good story fail. That is what this skill protects.

**Formats change every few weeks.** What worked a month ago may be copied to death now (since early November 2026 the "In this town" format
is flooded with one-click AI videos). Before a series, look at what went viral in the last 1-2 weeks in the same visual style
(Roblox Studio look vs. soft Blender renders are different worlds) and check these numbers against them.

## 1. Story
| Hits | Weak videos |
|---|---|
| A whole story: named people, exact numbers, dialogue, one big event, an ending that flips the start | a moment or a list of weird things |
| First line: an impossible rule said as normal ("In this town, ..."); very often "... except me" | a vague setup ("this is a story about ...") |
| Details planted early come back at the end | nothing returns |
| Genres right now: eerie/horror, rule worlds, dark hero stories, warm emotional stories, classic tales retold in Roblox | random events |
| Length follows the story: 56-180 s, median ~100 s | too short to land, or long without escalation |
| Comment bait that fits the rule ("type your name the way my town says it": 35 k comments on 624 k likes), "Part 2 soon" | no reason to comment |

## 2. Hook (first 3 seconds)
- A character big, sharp and readable by 1.5 s: 11 of 12 hits, 0 of 5 weak videos. Best from frame 0.
- The rule is visible on that character at once (readable with sound off).
- Clean frame: subject centred, nothing in front of it. Open at medium distance (head 10-15 % of the frame height) or close and pull back within ~1 s.
- Weak openings: an empty place, a dark frame, tiny people, an object, two close-ups in a row, an aerial shot with a tree in front.

## 3. Places and camera
| Hits | Weak videos |
|---|---|
| Few places, revisited from new angles: 49-83 % of cuts stay in the same place | a new place almost every cut: 18-33 % |
| The camera travels through a place like a drone: 34-73 % of the time in moves of 3 s or longer, camera moving 82-96 % of the time (Studio-style hits) | similar numbers - camera motion alone does not separate them |
| Shot length median 1.4-2.6 s; one visible change per sentence | static stretches over 3 s, or cuts without reason |
| Shot sizes mixed: wide for new places and body comedy, medium as default, close for punchlines and key reactions (about a third of shots in drama hits, 7-15 % in everyday and comedy hits), inserts of objects (an eye, a clock) | everything the same distance, or one face in close-up sentence after sentence |
| A player-camera look: wide lens, often above head height looking down, the place and its people in the picture; two or more people in 25-60 % of shots | one person alone in the frame at eye level with a long lens (author's test: far too close, the town felt dead) |
| The viewer feels part of the town: characters face each other, the camera stands inside the scene | characters pose to the camera |

## 4. World, light, colour
| Hits | Weak videos |
|---|---|
| Places that look like real Roblox games (built from good Creator Store kits), with props that tell the story and text in the world (signs, calendars, clocks, phone screens) | empty grey default suburb, or self-made pastel blocks (reads as a toy world) |
| Every place has its own people doing what the place is for (customers queue and eat, coworkers work, people wait at the stop), plus walkers and cars outside | dead places with only the hero in them, random people, or crowds that cheer and dance into the lens |
| One mood light per place; warm practical lights (windows, lamps); night, dusk and warm interiors are normal (brightness 0.27-0.55) | flat light, or everything dark and muddy on a phone |
| One colour world per place; saturation 0.45-0.75 inside it | grey and washed out (0.29-0.31), or many competing hues and neon (reads as shrill) |

## 5. Characters and faces
| Hits | Weak videos |
|---|---|
| A recurring hero per channel with one signature look (a cap, a hoodie in one colour with one emblem), the same in every video | a new random avatar every time |
| Characters look like real Roblox players of today (current avatar fashion); side roles read from one piece (chef hat, police cap, top hat) | default or random catalog outfits, patterned shirts with text |
| Each character has an attitude: its default face is its personality (bored, smug, calm grandma smile) | everyone with the same friendly smile |
| 1 hero plus 1-2 partners on screen most of the time | 15 new faces in 50 shots |
| Simple, readable faces (dot or oval eyes, strong brows, simple mouth); big expressions only at turning points | shock and trauma faces all the time (author's test: 59 % shock faces felt far too traumatic) |

## 5b. Graphics
| Hits | Weak videos |
|---|---|
| Small comic stickers on the words: speed lines, "ZZZ", "???", a clock, "1 HOUR LATER", labels on doors and signs | nothing but captions, or big text panels instead of action |

## 6. Captions, voice, sound
- Captions in every hit, every word in sync. Two styles: small bold text in the lower third, 1-3 words (most recent Studio-style hits),
  or one big word in the middle (soft-render channels). Stressed word in yellow.
- Narrator in most current hits: lively, 3.5-4.7 words per second, no pause over 0.8 s, median pitch 170-213 Hz. A flat, eerie narrator
  (where the format started) is a style choice; a flat voice by accident just sounds dull.
- Never silent: about -14 to -16 LUFS, music or room sound 8-12 dB under the voice, sound effects on visible events.

## 7. What screams "low effort" (avoid)
- Flickering bodies, characters see-through for a frame, hair layers popping (half-transparent body parts in Roblox).
- Blurry characters in front of a sharp background (a depth-of-field effect left on), crawling edges.
- The camera inside a tree, a wall or a person; a shoulder filling the frame; long turns that show parked cars and empty pavement.
- Default animation loops for everything; characters sliding; extras dancing in the background.
- Effects that are busy and confusing; more detail is not better (author's test: the biggest lesson of the whole project).
