# Scripts: what makes these Shorts work

Principles distilled from a private study of 58 narrated blocky-world Shorts (13 channels, each channel's last 40
Shorts read for its median). "Viral" and "weak" below are relative to each channel's own median views. Samples are
small: treat every number as a direction, not a law. All examples here are our own.

## 1. The hook (first 2 seconds)
- **The first word is spoken at 0.0 s.** No intro, no logo, no title card (median first word 0.0 s).
- **State the world's rule in the first sentence** (about two thirds of the stories do): "In this world, …" is the most
  common form; "In this town / my town / my world, …" are variants.
- **Show the rule in the picture at the same time**, with the hero's face in frame. A detail shot without a face (a
  shoe, a hand) did not read as a hook in our tests.
- Our examples:
  - "In this world, flowers grow wherever you step… but only when you're happy."
  - "In this world, your socks change colour every time you change your mind."
  - "In my town, every lie you tell adds one brick to your backpack."

## 2. The world's rule
- **A rule about something every viewer has** (a first word, a mood, a voice, a birthday, a shadow) lets the viewer
  stand inside it. Rules where the narrator is an object ("I'm a sock") or that live in a far fantasy kingdom did worse
  inside the same channel.
- One rule, simple enough to say in one breath, with a **visible** effect (something grows, glows, appears over the
  head, changes colour). If the rule can't be drawn, it can't be read muted.
- Put a **named friend or rival at stake**: the hero risks or loses something for someone (most of the strongest stories
  in the best channel had one; none of its weakest did).

## 3. Structure and timing
| beat | where | notes |
|---|---|---|
| rule + hero | 0–10% | the rule and the hero's place in it |
| escalation | from ~15–40% | the rule causes trouble, stakes rise |
| **twist** | median **~70%** of the runtime (range 53–81% in the best channel's hits) | the rule flips who wins, or shows its hidden cost |
| second movement | after the twist | the best long ones keep 35–70 s after the twist for a rescue, a choice or a consequence |
| ending | last 5–10% | **back on the rule** (44 of 58 endings call back to the hook); tone mostly warm or funny, rarely dark |

- **Pace: ~260 words per minute**, first person in most stories, short sentences, a joke or turn every few seconds.
- **Two lengths work:** long rule films (100–180 s, 520–750 words) and short pieces (17–50 s, 70–230 words). In the
  channel with the clearest data, every weak film was shorter than its shortest hit (fewer words at the same speed):
  length that comes from more story, not slower talk.
- Leave the picture running ~0.5–0.9 s after the last word.

## 4. Titles
- **Concept noun + one emoji** (1–3 words, no hashtags) is the cleanest form, e.g. "Happy Steps 🌼", "Mood Socks 🧦".
- **The rule stated**, sometimes trailing "…": "Lying Adds Bricks… 🧱" (with 2–3 hashtags on smaller channels).
- **A personal tease** in first person: "I Dropped My Ice Cream 😳". Most such titles in the sample were above their
  channel's median.
- **Avoid:** titles that spoil the twist, all-lower-case or misspelt titles (all such titles in the sample were weak),
  credit tags ("cr: @someone") instead of a promise, a question glued to many hashtags, and a moral spelled out in 15
  words.

## 5. Why copies of a successful channel fail
Borrowing the hook phrase and the title shape is not enough. The copies in the sample were 175–250 words long (the
original's films 430–750), had no bond built before the stakes, no payoff that returns to the rule (one stopped
mid-scene), credit-tag titles, and fewer cuts (about 4 per 10 s instead of 6–7). Copy principles, never content: our
rules, characters and stories must be original.

## 6. A silent test (like the template)
Without a voice the same grammar applies: the rule must read in the first second (an effect you can see), something
happens by ~30–40%, the twist lands by ~60–70%, and the last shot shows the rule's result for both sides in one frame.
The template's 10 s story: steps grow flowers → he trips, the top scoop flies → it lands in a sad girl's empty cone →
her ground blooms, his greys and wilts → over-the-shoulder two-shot of both.
